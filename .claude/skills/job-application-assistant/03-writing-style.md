# Writing Style

Applies to every document this workspace produces: cover letters, statements,
emails, and the prose parts of a CV.

## Critical rules

- **No fabrication.** Every claim traces to `01-candidate-profile.md`, the master CV
  in `documents/cv/`, or a statement in `documents/statements/`. A fact confirmed in
  conversation and not written into the profile does not exist on the next run.
- **No em-dashes.** Use a comma, a colon, or a full stop.
- **No cliches.** "Passionate about", "proven track record", "world-class",
  "cutting-edge", "I am writing to express my interest".
- **No apologetic hedging.** "I believe I could perhaps contribute" says nothing.
  State the thing, or state the gap.
- **First person, active voice.** "I estimate", not "estimation was performed".
- **A gap is stated once, plainly, and framed** - never repeated, never hidden.

## Register for a search committee

- Committees read for evidence, not enthusiasm. A named paper beats an adjective.
- Name the department's own work when you have verified it. Naming nothing reads as
  a form letter; naming something wrong is worse than naming nothing.
- Say what a result means, not only that it exists: "the elasticity estimates change
  sign once censoring is handled" carries more than "I study demand systems".
- Numbers where the record has them: enrolments, grant amounts, effect sizes.
- No exclamation marks. No rhetorical questions.

## Statements specifically

- A research statement is organised around a question, not a chronology of papers.
- A teaching statement earns its claims with a course, a student, or a decision you
  made in a classroom.
- Both are written for a reader outside your subfield: the committee has one
  specialist and four generalists.

## Language

- The cover letter matches the posting's language. The CV language is a profile-level
  choice and stays constant across postings.
- When writing in a second language, keep sentences short and do not translate an
  idiom.
